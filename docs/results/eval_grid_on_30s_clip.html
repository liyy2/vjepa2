What num_segments=12 · frames_per_clip=32 · frame_step=1 actually does on a 30 s clip

This is the configuration the dense V-JEPA cache uses. On a 10 s clip it over-samples with heavy overlap; on a 30 s clip it under-samples and leaves 57% of the video unseen. Below is the math and a diagram.

1. The math

Inputs given to the multi-clip sampler:

ParameterValueMeaning
num_segments12Number of independent forward passes per video
frames_per_clip32Frames per forward pass
frame_step1Stride within a pass: 1 = consecutive frames
tubelet_size2Temporal token = 2 adjacent frames
fps30Video frame rate

One segment covers 32 frames × 1 stride / 30 fps ≈ 1.07 s of footage and produces 16 temporal tokens after tubelet pooling. The full video is then chopped into 12 such segments whose starts are placed uniformly across the available range. With L = total frames, segments are spaced by (L − 32) / 11 frames between consecutive starts.

3. Why this matters for UPDRS 3.4 (finger tapping)

The clinical scoring criteria for item 3.4 require observation of ≥10 taps with explicit attention to speed, amplitude, hesitations, and amplitude decrement. Decrement is by definition a multi-second phenomenon. With this eval grid:

Per segment (within 1.07 s)Across the 30 s clip
Taps observed @ 3 Hz~3 taps~36 taps in segments + ~55 in gaps
Tubelets / cycle~5 tubelets per tap (67 ms each)—
Decrement visible withinImpossible — too few tapsIn principle yes, but the head only sees segment N's pooled summary
The structural problem. Within one segment (~3 taps), the model cannot measure decrement — there aren't enough cycles to compare. Across segments, the information about "tap amplitude in segment N vs segment N+11" would have to survive (a) the encoder's mean spatial pool, (b) the gap of 1.56 s between adjacent segments, and (c) the downstream pooling. Even if (a) and (c) are fixed by an order-aware combiner, (b) loses 57% of the clip outright.

The break-even point is around 13.5 s. Below that, segments overlap (redundant info, doesn't hurt accuracy). Above that, gaps open up and grow linearly.

5. What this implies for our results

Earlier diagnostics (from adaptive_window_item_3_4_fold0_status.md) split the fold-0 val into length bins on the old dataset:

BinAccQWK
≤ 8.5 s75.0%0.816
8.5–14 s45.7%0.385
> 14 s25.0%0.308

QWK drops by 0.5 from the short-clip bin to the long-clip bin — almost the entire gap to kinematic. A meaningful portion of the V-JEPA ceiling has nothing to do with feature quality; it's the eval-grid losing the long clips.

Two clean fixes for the 30 s case:
  • Adaptive windows: set num_segments = ⌈duration_s⌉ instead of 12. For a 30 s clip that's 30 windows × 32 frames @ stride 1 = 960 frames at stride spacing 28.6 ≈ 100% coverage. Already implemented in ClipDataset via adaptive_num_clips=True.
  • Bigger frame_step for long clips: frame_step=3 turns each 32-frame pass into 32 × 3 / 30 ≈ 3.2 s of footage. 12 such passes cover 38.4 s of "footage-time" — exceeds the 30 s clip, restoring full coverage.
Both are already supported by cache_vjepa21_temporal_embeddings.py. We've used frame_step=2 (the "coarse" cache) and adaptive windows in earlier experiments. Neither closed the QWK gap on may19 fold 0, but both are valid mitigations for the long-clip subset specifically.
