54 configurations tried (6 caches × 3 reducers × 3 model classes), 756 R² measurements. Verdict: V-JEPA cannot reliably predict the rhythm-critical kinematic features. Some bandpower/peak-count features are weakly predictable, but decrement/amplitude/velocity remain deeply negative.

Observations:

  • LightGBM dominates Ridge/MLP in every cache. Tree-based non-linearity exploits the few features V-JEPA can partially encode.
  • Coarser temporal sampling helps slightly — adaptive_step2_max24 (2.13 s/window) and coarse_step2 are the top performers. Larger windows have more motion to encode.

The pattern is clear. V-JEPA features can partially predict spectral power (the "is there motion in this band" signal — band powers and peak counts get R² ≈ 0.10). That's appearance-of-motion. V-JEPA features cannot predict decrement, amplitude, or velocity magnitude — the actual physical scale of motion over time. These are precisely the features that UPDRS scoring depends on.

4. Why band power succeeds and amplitude fails

Band power (0.5–8 Hz) reflects "how regularly is something oscillating in this video." A pretrained encoder with general motion priors picks up "there are oscillations at ~3 Hz here" because the spatial appearance changes periodically.

Decrement and amplitude require encoding the magnitude of motion — how big each tap is, and whether subsequent taps are smaller. The V-JEPA encoder doesn't have a "tap size" axis in its representation. Its features describe what hand pose is present, not how widely the fingers separate. Without a ground-truth amplitude scale, the encoder can't produce a regression target that correlates with the MediaPipe-computed amplitude.

5. Conclusion for direction (1)

V-JEPA features genuinely cannot supply the kinematic information. All 54 (cache × reducer × model) combinations have negative mean R². The features that ARE partially predictable (band power, peak count) only carry ~10% of the variance — they explain "is motion present" but not "how is the motion changing." This is not a head, aggregation, or fine-tuning issue. It's an encoder-output issue, and confirms in detail the earlier R²=−1.22 number.

6. Pivot to direction (2): hybrid kin + V-JEPA

Since the V-JEPA features do capture some band-power / peak-count signal that's not literally identical to the kinematic features, fusing them with the kinematic features might still yield a modest gain — V-JEPA may pick up on signals the MediaPipe-feature LightGBM misses (e.g., overall hand-pose appearance during high-effort taps, contextual cues across the whole frame).

The next experiment, train_hybrid_kin_vjepa.py:

  1. Train kinematic-only LightGBM (the existing baseline, QWK 0.728).
  2. Get V-JEPA combiner's val_probs (best of multi-scale adaptive sweep, QWK 0.555).
  3. Sweep fusion weight α ∈ [0, 1] of probability average.
  4. Report ceiling QWK as upper bound; then run the honest version with subject-OOF inner CV for the V-JEPA train predictions.

If even val-tuned fusion fails to beat 0.728, the kinematic LightGBM is genuinely the better choice on this dataset. If it gets to ~0.76+, the honest subject-OOF version is worth running.
