What V-JEPA is actually learning — and why it misses tap rhythm

A walk through the current finger-tap pipeline (item 3.4) at the level of frames, tubelets, masks, and loss terms. The goal is to make the phrase "appearance ≠ rhythm" precise enough to act on, and to show why doubling the dataset or adding more attentive-probe blocks will not, by themselves, fix it.

1. Two different "clips" — don't conflate them

The word "clip" is doing too much work in this project. There are two stages, and "clip" means different things in each.

Stage 1 — SSL training

One "clip" = a 32-frame window at stride 1, i.e. ~1.07 s at 30 fps.
One such window is sampled randomly per video per iteration.
The model has to reconstruct masked tokens inside this one window.

Stage 2 — eval / attentive probe

The full hand-task video (~10 s) is split into 12 segments.
Each segment is itself a 32-frame, ~1.07 s sub-clip.
Each sub-clip is encoded independently; logits are averaged across the 12 passes.

Below I'll keep calling the 1.07 s thing a sub-clip and the 10 s thing a video.

2. Stage 1: V-JEPA self-supervised training

From configs/train_2_1/pd_hand/vitl384-lora-jepa-adapt-32f-step1-fold0.yaml:

frames_per_clip: 32
frame_step:      1
tubelet_size:    2
num_clips:       1
random_clip_sampling: true
allow_clip_overlap:   false
  • 32 frames at stride 1 = ~1.07 s of footage @ 30 fps.
  • num_clips: 1 — one random window per video per iteration.
  • random_clip_sampling: true — start position varies; over training the model sees many different 1-second windows from each video, but never two at once.
  • allow_clip_overlap: false — irrelevant here because num_clips=1, but signals "don't allow temporally adjacent samples in the same batch".

3. What a "clip" looks like as tokens

V-JEPA doesn't see pixels directly — it sees tubelet tokens. Plugging in the config:

So one V-JEPA window is a stack of 16 temporal slices, each containing 576 spatial patches. Each temporal slice corresponds to ~67 ms of footage — short enough that a fast finger tap (say 4 Hz, 250 ms per cycle) spans ~3–4 temporal tokens.

4. The loss term: predict masked tokens from visible ones

The objective is the V-JEPA masked-prediction loss. Two mask configs are mixed (from the same training config):

mask 1: 8 small spatial blocks  (~15% spatial area each), temporal_scale [1.0, 1.0]
mask 2: 2 large spatial blocks  (~70% spatial area each), temporal_scale [1.0, 1.0]

Both masks have temporal_scale [1.0, 1.0] — they cover all 16 temporal positions for whatever spatial region they pick. So masks look like vertical "columns" in the (space, time) token grid:

The loss says: "Look at the visible tokens (encoder output). Now produce embeddings for the masked locations that match the embeddings the EMA encoder would have produced if it had seen them." Loss = L1 distance (per config: loss_exp: 1.0) between predicted and target embeddings, averaged over masked positions.

Two things matter about that statement:

  1. "Match the EMA encoder's embedding" — not "match the pixels." The target is itself a learned representation that has been drifting alongside the student. The model only has to produce the same kind of feature that the EMA encoder produces for that location. If the EMA encoder mostly encodes appearance, the loss is satisfied by reproducing appearance.
  2. The masked region is local in space, global in time — the model must extrapolate spatially (from the rest of the hand) but is given no temporal extrapolation pressure outside the 1.07 s window.

6. Do the 12 segments overlap?

Almost always, yes, for the videos we have. With num_segments=12, frames_per_clip=32, frame_step=1:

Video lengthTotal framesSegment starts spaced byOverlap?
5 s150~11 frames (0.37 s)heavy overlap
10 s (typical)300~22 frames (0.74 s)overlap (~10 frames shared per pair)
13 s390~32 frames (1.07 s)borderline, no overlap, no gap
20 s600~52 frames (1.73 s)gaps between segments
30+ s900+≥ 79 frames (2.6 s+)large gaps; most of the video unseen

For 10-second clips this is fine — every frame is in at least one segment, and adjacent segments share content. For videos longer than ~13 s, segments start gapping, and the model literally never looks at the frames in those gaps. This is exactly the length-bin degradation in adaptive_window_item_3_4_fold0_status.md:

The "adaptive windows" track (set K = ceil(duration_s) instead of 12) was an attempt to close those gaps. It did — but it didn't move the QWK number, because gap-closing is not the bottleneck for typical-length clips.

7. Why "appearance ≠ rhythm" — the structural argument

Put the three observations together:

Observation A. The SSL loss rewards: reproducing the EMA encoder's embedding at a masked spatial location, given the visible spatial neighborhood, for all 16 timesteps. It is solvable using mostly appearance + slow drift; counting taps is not necessary.
Observation B. Each forward pass sees only ~1.07 s of footage. A typical PD finger-tap is 1.5–4 Hz, so 1.07 s contains ≈ 1–4 taps. Decrement (the canonical UPDRS 3.4 cue) is a property of the trajectory over ≥ 10 taps ≈ 5–10 s. It is not visible inside one V-JEPA window.
Observation C. At eval, the 12 per-window logit vectors are averaged. Averaging is a commutative operation: it does not know the order of the windows. So even if a given window encoded "I am the 3rd tap and I am 12% smaller than the 1st tap," that information would be flattened by the mean.

So the chain is:

  1. The SSL objective does not force the encoder to represent tap rhythm.
  2. Even if it did, no single window contains enough taps to see decrement.
  3. Even if some windows did encode that, the eval averages them in logit space, throwing away ordering.

That is the precise sense in which V-JEPA "encodes appearance, not rhythm" in our current setup. It's not a property of V-JEPA the algorithm — it's a property of how we've configured it for this task. Three independent steps each strip out rhythm.

Corollary that matches the experimental results. This explains why the cached-embedding → temporal-statistics → tabular-stacker recipe worked (QWK 0.689) while the learned aggregators collapsed (QWK 0.0–0.2). The stacker computes across-window statistics (mean, std, percentiles of per-window embeddings) and a gradient-boosted classifier learns "this video's per-window-mean embedding has high std → unsteady → severity 2". That's the first place in the pipeline where between-window order / variability becomes a signal. The learned aggregators had to discover this with ~200 examples and didn't.

8. UPDRS 3.4 vs the model's actual time horizon

  • Score on: speed (tap rate), amplitude (how far open), hesitations / freezing, and amplitude decrement (does the tap shrink over time).

Recommended sequence:
  1. Cheap test of Observation C: add temporal-derivative + FFT features to the cached-stats stacker, rerun fold 0. If QWK jumps, rhythm is in the embeddings and we were just throwing it away.
  2. Cheap test of Observation B: rerun the attentive eval with frame_step=2 (covers ~2.1 s per window). If QWK jumps, time horizon was the bottleneck.
  3. Then spend effort on motion inputs or kinematic distillation — informed by which of (1) and (2) moved the needle.
Both (1) and (2) are cheap, give independent diagnostic information, and don't commit to a major architecture change before the data tells us where the loss is being spent.

10. Footnote — the may19 dataset

The new segments_meeting_tasks_720p30_may19/finger_tapping dataset roughly doubles the clip count (671 vs 320 ok rows) and the subject count (308 vs 154). That changes the calculus for the "learned aggregator" approaches that previously collapsed at N≈200: a real cross-window attention head over 12 per-window embeddings might become learnable at N≈600.

It does not change the analysis above — V-JEPA's pretext task still doesn't reward encoding rhythm, regardless of how many videos we train on. More data lets us learn a pooler on top, not a new encoder. To actually push the encoder, one of the objective- or input-level changes is still required.
