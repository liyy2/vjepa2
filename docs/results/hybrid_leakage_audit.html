Leakage audit: the +0.030 hybrid lift does not survive a leak-free pipeline

In response to "no cheating and the results are publishable?" — yes, the originally-reported +0.030 lift had two hidden val-tuning leaks. After removing both, V-JEPA does not improve the clean kinematic baseline. The honest finding is a small negative effect (−0.012 mean QWK, 4/10 seed wins, not statistically significant).

Verdict. Direction (2) ("kin + V-JEPA outperforms kin alone") does NOT hold under a leak-free protocol on may19 fold 0. The previously-reported +0.030 mean lift (Wilcoxon p=0.003) was driven by:
  1. The cached V-JEPA features came from a LoRA-FT encoder checkpoint best.pt selected by outer-val QWK (val-tuned encoder weights);
  2. The combiner's OOF predictions for both train and val were taken at the epoch with peak val QWK (val-tuned snapshot).
When both leaks are removed (use latest.pt, train combiner with no early stopping on either inner_val or outer_val), the mean Δ flips to −0.012, with 4/10 seed wins and Wilcoxon p=0.90.

1. The two leaks identified

Leak A — LoRA-FT backbone selected by outer-val QWK

The supervised LoRA-FT pipeline writes two checkpoints during training:

CheckpointSelection ruleLeak status
best.ptepoch with highest outer-val QWKleaky
latest.ptfinal epoch of training (no selection)clean

The original cache pipeline merged best.pt (epoch 16 of 30, val QWK 0.499) into the base V-JEPA weights. The encoder produced for the cache therefore has weights chosen using outer-val labels.

Leak B — Combiner predictions taken at peak val QWK

The combiner used for OOF stacking has early stopping with patience 80, returning the model state at the epoch with highest val QWK. Two subtler points:

  • For the 5 inner folds: the combiner selects best by inner_val QWK. Downstream ExtraTrees then learns on (inner_val features, inner_val label) pairs — implicitly the V-JEPA features were optimized using those labels.
  • For the full-train combiner → outer val predictions: the combiner selects best by outer-val QWK. The 5 prob values used as features for the outer-val row are the snapshot from the epoch that scored highest on that same row's label.

Leak B for the full-train combiner is the most direct contamination. Even with subject-stratified outer folds, picking the best snapshot by outer-val QWK adds information from outer-val labels into the predictions.

2. The fix

Two changes:

  1. Use the LoRA-FT latest.pt (final epoch 30, no val selection). Re-merge with the same base V-JEPA weights and re-cache the adaptive features.
  2. Pass --no-val-tuning to the OOF combiner script. This disables early stopping for both inner folds and the full-train combiner. Predictions are taken at the final epoch (epoch 79 of 80) for every model.

Code change in scripts/pd_hand/oof_vjepa_combiner_predictions.py:

def train_combiner_and_predict(..., select_on_val=True):
    last_state = {"probs": probs.copy(), "qwk": float(qwk), "epoch": epoch}
    if select_on_val:
        # original best-by-val-QWK selection
        if qwk > best_qwk:
            best_qwk = qwk
            best_state = {"probs": probs.copy(), ...}
            epochs_since = 0
        else:
            ...
    if select_on_val:
        return best_state
    return last_state  # final-epoch prediction, no val tuning

4. What's still sound

The leakage in the hybrid result does not affect the rest of the experimental story:

  • unaffected Outer subject-stratified 5-fold split. Built once by prepare_minimal_hand_vjepa2.py. No subject appears in both train and val.
  • unaffected Inner StratifiedGroupKFold by subject_id. Verified independently. No subject overlap between inner train and inner val.
  • unaffected R² scan conclusion (direction 1). 54 cache×reducer×model combinations all give negative mean R² predicting kinematic scalars. That analysis used Ridge / MLP / LightGBM with their own internal validation, not outer val.
  • unaffected Frozen V-JEPA combiner / tstats QWK ceiling (~0.53). The combiner sweeps used train-only training with combiner-internal early stopping (which still has leak B, but the headline number is the val score itself, not a stacked downstream — so it's a real upper bound rather than a contaminated mean).
  • unaffected kin+dx baseline at 0.559. ExtraTrees has no early stopping; the baseline is a pure train→val fit.
  • unaffected The published kinematic baseline old_118 (QWK 0.725). The model selection among the 28 (feature_set × model) candidates IS val-tuned (the baseline picks "best by val QWK"), but that's how the original 0.725 number is reported and it's used the same way in both leaky and leak-free comparisons here.

5. Why the leak was big enough to flip the sign

Two factors:

  1. Small val set (n=107) means QWK fluctuates by ~0.05 across epochs even for a stable encoder. Picking the best-by-val-QWK epoch effectively pre-selects the epoch where random noise aligns with val labels.
  2. Combiner QWK is highly noisy across epochs. Looking at the V-JEPA combiner training logs: val QWK can move ±0.05 epoch-to-epoch. Best-by-val-QWK selection grabs 0.04 of that noise as "signal" — exactly comparable to the +0.030 mean lift we saw.

So +0.030 of "lift" was within the variability that best-by-val-QWK selection can manufacture on an n=107 validation set. The honest 10-seed comparison detected it (p=0.003) because the SAME val-tuned snapshot was used across all 10 seeds — the leak is a constant offset in the V-JEPA features, picked up consistently by ExtraTrees.

6. What's required for a publishable positive result

If the goal is "kin + V-JEPA outperforms kin alone" reportable in a paper, the pipeline needs:

  1. LoRA-FT encoder selection by held-out-of-train subject group. Split fold-0 train into subject-OOF inner sub-folds. Use one as inner-val for LoRA-FT early stopping; never touch outer-val until final report.
  2. Combiner training with the same protocol. Same inner-sub-fold for early stopping inside each OOF inner fold.
  3. 5-fold outer CV reporting. Single fold 0 has too few val clips (n=107) to be reliable. Mean ± std over folds 1-5 with the same leak-free protocol is the right ground truth.
  4. Pre-registered selection of which V-JEPA cache and combiner config to use. The current best (coarse_step2 multi-scale frozen, vs LoRA-FT latest adaptive) was chosen partly because it gave the best leaky number. A pre-registration would freeze this choice.

Doable, but not free. Approximately ~24–36 GPU-hours of additional training to redo the LoRA-FT pipeline with inner-sub-fold early stopping on 5 outer folds.
